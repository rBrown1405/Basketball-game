# Animation research notes

The procedural animation in `js/match/` is tuned to measured human movement and NBA tracking data rather than
guessed. This file keeps the numbers the code targets and where they come from, so later tuning stays honest.

## Joint limits (rig.js)

Active ranges of healthy adults (AAOS goniometry norms): shoulder flexion 180 / extension 60, abduction 180,
rotation 70 / 90; elbow 0-150 (no more than ~5 deg hyperextension); wrist 80 / 70; hip flexion 120-125 /
extension 30, abduction 45, rotation 45; knee 0-135 (hyperextension 0-5); ankle 20 / 50; trunk 80 / 25 / 35 / 45;
neck ~50 / 60 / 40 / 70. https://goniometer.io/range-of-motion , https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22510944/ (arm swivel).

## Half-court movement (flow.js)

From NBA SportVU tracking (2015-16 sample) and NBA.com team tracking: passes ~5.4 per 24 s of half-court time
(Warriors ~6.7, Rockets ~4.8); ~1 rim cut every 8 s; a perimeter relocation every ~2.6 s; off-ball players spend
23% / 44% / 21% / 11% / 1% of the time under 2 / 2-6 / 6-10 / 10-15 / over 15 ft/s; nearest teammate ~14 ft;
idle spells rarely over 3 s. Passes per possession by system: motion / Princeton ~3-3.4, isolation ~2.4-2.6.
https://github.com/linouk23/NBA-Player-Movements , https://github.com/gabriel1200/site_Data

## Player models and rendering (human.js, human_build.js, gl3d.js)

The realistic players start from the MakeHuman 1.1 base mesh, targets and skeleton weights, which the MakeHuman
project released under CC0 1.0 (https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman, LICENSE.ASSETS.md).
`tools/human/build.js` converts them into `js/match/human_data.js`.

## Ball and net physics (ball.js, hoop.js)

| Topic | What the code does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Floor bounce | restitution by impact speed: ~0.77 at 19 ft/s (FIBA's drop test: 1.8 m, rebound to 1.035-1.085 m to the underside of the ball), falling ~0.013 per ft/s faster (tracking data over millions of NBA bounces), 0.70-0.84; a dropped ball bounces ten times or so before it rolls | https://assets.fiba.basketball/image/upload/documents-corporate-fiba-official-rules-2024-official-basketball-rules-and-basketball-equipment.pdf , https://www.inpredictable.com/2017/01/a-deflategate-analysis-for-nba.html |
| Spin at a bounce | rigid-body impulse with the ball as a thin shell (I = 2/3 mR^2): friction drives the contact point to rolling (tangential restitution ~0) unless it slides (impulse capped at mu (1 + e) v_n, mu 0.55 assumed; the ball-rim value identified from video is ~0.5); backspin makes a bounce check up, topspin skips on, and the spin after each bounce carries into the next | https://www.physics.sydney.edu.au/~cross/Gripslip.pdf , https://pubs.aip.org/aapt/ajp/article/73/10/914/1042412/Bounce-of-a-spinning-ball-near-normal-incidence , https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245481335_Identification_of_basketball_parameters_for_a_simulation_model |
| Air drag | a linear drag fit (0.15/s) standing in for Cd ~0.5: at shot speeds drag is 10-17 % of the ball's weight, so the descent is steeper than the rise (entry ~45-48 deg on a 50 deg jumper); passes fly with real gravity and drag (a quick pass nearly flat, a long one arcs) | https://www.inpredictable.com/2015/08/kind-of-drag.html , https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245481335_Identification_of_basketball_parameters_for_a_simulation_model |
| Backspin | shots leave the fingertips at 1.45-2.05 rev/s (measured 1.72 +/- 0.33 on jump shots and free throws) | https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2022.2080164 |
| Bank shots | off the glass the ball keeps ~70 % of its speed into the board (no direct measurement found; 0.65-0.75 used) and friction with the backspin adds downward speed; the spot on the board is solved so the carom drops through the middle of the rim | https://empiricalzeal.com/2014/04/23/basketball-physics/ |
| Rolling | around the rim: some makes land on the front of the ring and ride around it (centre just inside the ring, speed falling as friction takes it) before they drop; on the floor: rolling resistance until it stops, the spin always matching the travel | https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/dynamicsystems/article/128/2/359/478119/Dynamics-of-a-Basketball-Rolling-Around-the-Rim |
| Bounce passes | the floor contact is solved so the real bounce (restitution by impact speed, friction against the backspin) rises to the receiver's hands on time; it hits the floor a little past halfway and comes off slower | https://www.physics.sydney.edu.au/~cross/Gripslip.pdf |
| Net | nylon cloth: 12 strands tied to the ring, 7 rows of knots in a diamond mesh, 1.45 ft long (FIBA 400-450 mm, NBA 15-18 in), narrower than the ball near the bottom so it checks the ball as it passes; verlet integration with 3 substeps and 2 constraint passes; the ball pushes knots to its surface and drags them along a little (the ball moves the net, not the reverse); a stiffer top that cannot whip up over the ring; it snaps back and whips after a swish | https://assets.fiba.basketball/image/upload/documents-corporate-fiba-official-rules-2026-equipment-v1-0.pdf , https://patents.google.com/patent/US8000947B1/en , https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/academic/class/15462-s13/www/lec_slides/Jakobsen.pdf , https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/tenMinutePhysics/09-xpbd.pdf |
| Rim | a breakaway rim tips ~10 deg down at the front while a dunker hangs on it and springs back (the rule allows 10-30 deg); after a hit it wobbles, slower and more damped than the real ~24 Hz mode so it shows at 60 fps; the net hangs from the rim and moves with it | https://www.athleticbusiness.com/facilities/gym-fieldhouse/article/15140811/manufacturers-continue-to-improve-the-basketball-goal , https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202308.0671/v1 |

## Motion and contact fixes from the motion audit

| Topic | What the code does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sideways / backpedal gait | a defensive or offensive shuffle keeps the arms quiet (no jog arm swing at shuffle cadence) and the torso still; forward, sideways and backward gait parameters are blended by direction instead of switched | https://www.breakthroughbasketball.com/defense/Debunking-Cross-Feet , https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call |
| Body contact | capsule-style push-out on pelvis and chest (torsos lean ahead of the feet), a hard floor, and the closing velocity cancelled at contact so steering slides players around each other | https://www.red3d.com/cwr/papers/1999/gdc99steer.html , https://www.nature.com/articles/35035023 |

## Motion capture (tools/mocap, clips_mocap.js)

Real captures from the CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database (free for research and commercial use; BVH release by
Bruce Hahne) are retargeted onto the rig by `tools/mocap/retarget.js`: pelvis, spine, chest, neck and head from each
joint's rotation relative to the take's T-pose, arms and legs from bone directions (so the captured person's
proportions do not matter; limb directions match the capture to ~0.1 deg median), the facing from the pelvis
heading, the root from the pelvis's ground point, and the feet's real contacts turned into planted phases and
scripted steps (pivots become zero-lift steps about the ball of the foot) so planted feet never slide. The data used
in this project was obtained from mocap.cs.cmu.edu. The database was created with funding from NSF EIA-0196217.

## Transitions without pops (rig.js, actor.js, ball.js, choreo.js)

A per-frame tracer over live possessions (joint accelerations relative to the body, with what changed in the
frames before each spike) found the remaining pops came from switches, not from the motion itself: hand IK
turning on or off in one frame, grips and stances swapping, the shoulder IK flipping to its other equivalent
solution, landing spots re-predicted wildly, and airborne legs handed from IK to the clip at take-off.
